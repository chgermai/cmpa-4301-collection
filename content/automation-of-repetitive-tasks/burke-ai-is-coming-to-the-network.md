**Source:** Burke, J. (2026). *AI Is Coming to the Network.* Network World. https://www.networkworld.com/article/971784/ai-is-coming-to-the-network.html

**What it covers:** An analysis from an independent trade publication laying out four ways AI reshapes network operations work. This includes offloading repetitive tasks from experienced staff, training people into other roles, reducing role requirements so less-experienced staff can fill them, and eventually changing specialized network roles into generalist IT positions. The article ties this to an expected staffing shortage as experienced network engineers retire.

**Why it's valuable:** It's independent analysis rather than a vendor or product pitch, and it looks past the immediate "AI automates a task" framing to the longer-term staffing and career impact, which other, tool-focused sources don't address.

**Who would find it useful:** Engineering managers and team leads thinking about how automation changes staffing and hiring, not just about what gets automated.

**Limitations:** It's a forecast and opinion piece, not a study. The framework (offload/reskill/deskill/displace) is the author's own model, not something independently validated.
