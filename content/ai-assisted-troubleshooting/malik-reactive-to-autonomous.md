**Source:** Malik, A. (2026). *From Reactive to Autonomous: Evolution of AI Operations in Cloud Network Infrastructure.* arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14574

**What it covers:** This peer-reviewed paper proposes a five-generation framework for how cloud network operations teams move from manual, runbook-driven troubleshooting toward a fully automated and eventually autonomous process. Each generation is defined by how much of the diagnose-and-respond loop is handled without a human in the loop.

**Why it's valuable:** The author draws on experience running hyperscale networks, not a hypothetical model. The paper makes the case that progress toward autonomous operations requires more than better AI models — it requires changes in tooling, team structure, and culture. That framing is useful for making the case internally for why AI tooling is worth investing in, beyond just picking a product.

**Who would find it useful:** Anyone trying to build a roadmap for moving an existing alerting and troubleshooting platform toward an AI-centric model, especially at a level where you need to justify the investment to leadership.

**Limitations:** The framework is built around a generic hyperscale cloud environment. There's no discussion of how the generations apply to smaller, non-hyperscale environments, which is where a lot of teams actually operate.
